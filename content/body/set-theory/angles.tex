\chapter{Angles}

As opposed to being a measure, we define an angle as angle as a geometric figure, ie: a set of points. Specifically, an angle is the union of two rays that share a common vertex.
\begin{definition}[Angle]
    An \textbf{angle} is the union of two rays $\overrightarrow{AO}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ that share a common vertex $O$, denoted as follows
    \[
    \angle AOB \coloneqq \overrightarrow{AO} \cup \overrightarrow{OB}.
    \]
\end{definition}

Using the half-planes defined previously, we have the tools neccessary to describe the interior and exterior of an angle.
\begin{definition}[Interior of an Angle]
    For any three non-collinear points $A, B, C \in \mathbf{P}$, the \textbf{interior} of $\angle ABC$ is the set:
    \[
    \text{Int}(\angle ABC) \coloneqq \{ P \in \mathbf{P} \mid \mathcal{S}(P, A, \overleftrightarrow{BC}) \land \mathcal{S}(P, C, \overleftrightarrow{BA}) \}.
    \]
    Equivalently, this is the intersection of two half-planes:
    \[
    \text{Int}(\angle ABC) \coloneqq H_{\overleftrightarrow{BC}, A} \cap H_{\overleftrightarrow{BA}, C}.
    \]
\end{definition}

# Maloney's Geometry — Master Axiom & Theorem Reference

## 1. System Overview & Meta-Rules
Maloney's Geometry is an absolute (neutral), synthetic geometric system built strictly upon set theory and three primitive notions.

* **NO Distance Metric:** Do not introduce real numbers ($\mathbb{R}$), distance functions ($d(A,B)$), degrees, radians, or coordinate systems.
* **Allowed Constructions:** All proofs, definitions, and derivations must rely strictly on points ($\mathbf{P}$), betweenness ($\mathcal{B}$), congruence ($\cong$), same-side relations ($\mathcal{S}$), half-planes ($H$), and standard set-theoretic operations ($\cup, \cap, \setminus, \subset, \emptyset$).
* **Representative Independence:** Because equivalence relations partition sets into distinct classes, any point inside a half-plane or line can serve as its representative name.

---

## 2. Primitive Notions & Domain Structures

* **Points:** The universal base set of all geometric points, denoted $\mathbf{P}$.
* **Betweenness:** A ternary relation on points, denoted $\mathcal{B}(A,B,C)$, read as "$B$ lies strictly between $A$ and $C$." Formally: $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathbf{P} \times \mathbf{P} \times \mathbf{P}$.
* **Congruence:** An equivalence relation on pairs of points (segments), denoted $\cong$, indicating spatial equivalence. Formally: $\cong \subseteq (\mathbf{P} \times \mathbf{P}) \times (\mathbf{P} \times \mathbf{P})$.
* **The Plane:** The fundamental mathematical structure $\mathcal{P} \coloneqq \langle \mathbf{P}, \mathcal{B}, \cong \rangle$.
* **Set of All Lines ($\mathbf{L}$):** The family of all subsets of $\mathbf{P}$ generated by two distinct points:
  $$\mathbf{L} \coloneqq \{ l \subseteq \mathbf{P} \mid \exists A,B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \land l = \overleftrightarrow{AB} \}$$
* **Geometric Figure:** Any proper subset of the point set, $F \subseteq \mathbf{P}$.

## 3. Fundamental Axioms

### Existence & Point Axioms
* **Existence Axiom:** There exist at least two distinct points in the plane:
  $$\exists A,B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B$$

### Axioms of Betweenness
1. **Ax 1 (Symmetry of Betweenness):** The order of outer endpoints can be reversed:
   $$\mathcal{B}(A,B,C) \implies \mathcal{B}(C,B,A)$$
2. **Ax 2 (Order of Betweenness):** A point cannot be between a sequence while also being an endpoint:
   $$\mathcal{B}(A,B,X) \implies \neg\mathcal{B}(B,A,X) \land \neg\mathcal{B}(A,X,B)$$
3. **Ax 3 (Identity/Reflexivity of Betweenness):** Betweenness collapses if endpoints coincide:
   $$\mathcal{B}(A,B,A) \implies A = B$$
4. **Ax 4 (Density of Points / Inner Point):** Between any two distinct points lies another point:
   $$A \neq C \implies \exists B \in \mathbf{P} : \mathcal{B}(A,B,C)$$
5. **Ax 5 (Extensibility / Outer Point):** Any line segment can be extended indefinitely beyond its endpoint:
   $$A \neq B \implies \exists C \in \mathbf{P} : \mathcal{B}(A,B,C)$$
6. **Ax 6 (Transitivity of Betweenness):** Linear sequences chain logically:
   $$\mathcal{B}(A,B,C) \land \mathcal{B}(B,C,D) \land B \neq C \implies \mathcal{B}(A,B,D)$$

### Axioms of Congruence
7. **Ax 7 (Reversal of Congruence):** Segment orientation does not alter congruence:
   $$AB \cong BA$$
8. **Ax 8 (Transitivity of Congruence):** Congruence is transitive across pairs:
   $$AB \cong CD \land AB \cong EF \implies CD \cong EF$$
9. **Ax 9 (Identity of Congruence):** Null segments imply identical points (excludes zero-distance universe):
   $$AB \cong CC \implies A = B$$

### Axioms of Interaction & Construction
10. **Ax 11 (Segment Construction / Copying):** A segment can be laid down along any ray starting from any point:
    $$\forall Q, X, A, B \in \mathbf{P}: Q \neq X \implies \exists Y \in \mathbf{P} : \mathcal{B}(Q,X,Y) \land XY \cong AB$$
11. **Ax 12 (Midpoint Existence):** Every line segment has a midpoint:
    $$\forall A,B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \implies \exists M \in \mathbf{P} : \mathcal{B}(A,M,B) \land AM \cong MB$$
12. **Ax 13 (Midpoint Uniqueness):** A segment cannot have more than one midpoint:
    $$\forall A,B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \implies \forall M,N \in \mathbf{P} : (AM \cong MB \land \mathcal{B}(A,M,B) \land AN \cong NB \land \mathcal{B}(A,N,B)) \implies M = N$$
13. **Ax 15 (Plane Separation Axiom - PSA):** Every line $l \in \mathbf{L}$ partitions the remainder of the plane into two disjoint sets $H_{l,A}, H_{l,B} \subset \mathbf{P} \setminus l$ such that:
    * **PSA 1 (Exhaustive & Disjoint):** $H_{l,A} \cup H_{l,B} = \mathbf{P} \setminus l \land H_{l,A} \cap H_{l,B} = \emptyset$
    * **PSA 2 (Intersection Equivalence):** $\forall A, B \in \mathbf{P} \setminus l : (\exists X \in l, \mathcal{B}(A, X, B) \iff \{A, B\} \not\subset H_{l1} \land \{A, B\} \not\subset H_{l2})$
14. **Ax 16 (Playfair's Axiom / Parallel Postulate):** Through a point not on a line, there exists exactly one parallel line:
    $$\forall l \in \mathbf{L}, \forall P \in \mathbf{P} \setminus l, \exists! m \in \mathbf{L} : (P \in m \land m \parallel l)$$

## 4. Linear Notions: Definitions & Theorems

### Definitions
* **Line Segment ($AB$):** The endpoints and all interior points between them:
  $$\forall A, B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \implies AB \coloneqq \{A, B\} \cup \{ X \in \mathbf{P} \mid \mathcal{B}(A, X, B) \}$$
* **Ray ($\overrightarrow{AB}$):** The origin $A$, interior point $B$, all points between them, and all outward extensions beyond $B$:
  $$\forall A, B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \implies \overrightarrow{AB} \coloneqq \{A, B\} \cup \{X \in \mathbf{P} \mid \mathcal{B}(A, X, B) \lor \mathcal{B}(A, B, X)\}$$
* **Line ($\overleftrightarrow{AB}$):** The extension of a segment indefinitely in both directions:
  $$\forall A, B \in \mathbf{P}, A \neq B \implies \overleftrightarrow{AB} \coloneqq \{A, B\} \cup \{X \in \mathbf{P} \mid \mathcal{B}(X, A, B) \lor \mathcal{B}(A, X, B) \lor \mathcal{B}(A, B, X)\}$$

### Core Linear Theorems
* **Thm (Infinite Interior Points):** $\forall A,C \in \mathbf{P}$ with $A \neq C$, there exist infinitely many points strictly between $A$ and $C$.
* **Thm (Infinite Outward Extension):** $\forall A,B \in \mathbf{P}$ with $A \neq B$, there exist infinitely many points $C_1, C_2, C_3, \dots$ such that $\mathcal{B}(A,B,C_1), \mathcal{B}(A,C_1,C_2), \dots$
  * *Corollary:* The plane $\mathcal{P}$ necessarily contains an infinite number of points.
* **Thm (Segment Symmetry):** For any two points, $AB = BA$. (Proven via set commutative property and Symmetry of Betweenness).
* **Thm (Ray Asymmetry):** For distinct points $A \neq B$, opposite rays are distinct: $\overrightarrow{AB} \neq \overrightarrow{BA}$. (Proven via Outer Point Axiom and Order of Betweenness).
* **Thm (Union of Opposite Rays):** Two opposite rays sharing an endpoint form a complete line:
  $$\overleftarrow{AB} \cup \overrightarrow{AB} = \overleftrightarrow{AB}$$
* **Thm (Intersection of Opposite Rays):** Opposite rays sharing an origin intersect exclusively at their shared segment:
  $$\overleftarrow{AB} \cap \overrightarrow{AB} = AB$$
* **Thm (Line Symmetry):** Lines are direction-independent: $\overleftrightarrow{AB} = \overleftrightarrow{BA}$.

## 5. Midpoints, Bisectors & Orthogonality

### Definitions
* **Midpoint:** A point $M$ is the midpoint of segment $AB$ if and only if $\mathcal{B}(A,M,B) \land AM \cong MB$.
* **Perpendicular Bisector ($\ell_{AB}$):** The set of all points equidistant (congruent) from the endpoints of a segment:
  $$\ell_{AB} \coloneqq \{ X \in \mathbf{P} \mid AX \cong XB \}$$
  * *Properties:* By derivation, $\ell_{AB}$ is a straight line ($\in \mathbf{L}$) that passes through the unique midpoint $M$ and is orthogonal to line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$. $M$ is the *only* point in $\ell_{AB}$ that intersects segment $AB$.

## 6. Plane Separation, Half-Planes & Quotient Spaces

### Definitions
* **Same-Side Relation ($\mathcal{S}$):** Two points are on the same side of a line $l$ if the segment connecting them does not intersect $l$:
  $$\mathcal{S}(A, B, l) \iff \nexists X \in l : \mathcal{B}(A, X, B)$$
* **Opposite-Side Relation ($\mathcal{O}$):** Two points are on opposite sides of a line $l$ if their segment intersects $l$:
  $$\mathcal{O}(A, B, l) \iff \neg\mathcal{S}(A, B, l) \iff \exists X \in l : \mathcal{B}(A, X, B)$$
* **Half-Plane ($H_{l,A}$):** The equivalence class of all points on the same side of line $l$ as reference point $A$:
  $$H_{l,A} \coloneqq \{X \in \mathbf{P} \setminus l \mid \mathcal{S}(A, X, l)\}$$

### Separation Theorems
* **Thm (Same-Side Reflexivity):** $\forall l \in \mathbf{L}, \forall A \in \mathbf{P} \setminus l : \mathcal{S}(A, A, l)$. (By identity of betweenness, a point cannot be strictly between itself).
* **Thm (Same-Side Symmetry):** $\mathcal{S}(A, B, l) \iff \mathcal{S}(B, A, l)$. (Direct derivation from symmetry of betweenness).
* **Thm 29 (Same-Side Transitivity):** $\mathcal{S}(A, B, l) \land \mathcal{S}(B, C, l) \implies \mathcal{S}(A, C, l)$.
* **Thm 30 (Partition Theorem):** Because $\mathcal{S}$ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, it is a formal **Equivalence Relation**. By the Fundamental Theorem of Equivalence Relations, $\mathcal{S}$ partitions the plane minus the line ($\mathbf{P} \setminus l$) into disjoint equivalence classes.
  * *Quotient Space Insight:* The quotient set $(\mathbf{P} \setminus l) / \mathcal{S}$ has a cardinality of exactly 2.
  * *Representative Swapping:* If $B \in H_{l,A}$, then $H_{l,B} = H_{l,A}$. Any point in the half-plane can name the half-plane.
* **Boolean Algebra of Interiors:** Because half-planes are formal sets, complex geometric interiors are defined as set intersections:
  * *Interior of an Angle ($\angle ABC$):* $H_{\overleftrightarrow{AB}, C} \cap H_{\overleftrightarrow{BC}, A}$
  * *Interior of a Triangle ($\triangle ABC$):* $H_{\overleftrightarrow{AB}, C} \cap H_{\overleftrightarrow{BC}, A} \cap H_{\overleftrightarrow{AC}, B}$

## 7. Parallelism & Direction

### Definitions
* **Parallel Lines ($\parallel$):** Two lines are parallel if they are identical (inclusive convention) or share no points in common:
  $$\forall l,m \in \mathbf{L}, \quad l \parallel m \iff (l = m) \lor (l \cap m = \emptyset)$$
  * *Note on Inclusive Convention:* Allowing $l \parallel l$ makes parallelism a reflexive equivalence relation. The equivalence classes of lines under $\parallel$ represent geometric **directions**.

### Parallelism Theorems
* **Thm (Reflexivity of Parallelism):** $\forall l \in \mathbf{L}, l \parallel l$.
* **Thm (Symmetry of Parallelism):** $l \parallel m \implies m \parallel l$.
* **Thm (Transitivity of Parallelism):** $(l \parallel m \land m \parallel n) \implies l \parallel n$.
  * *Proof Strategy:* Assumes $l \neq n$ and $l \cap n \neq \emptyset$ (sharing point $P$). By Playfair's Axiom (Ax 16), there can only be one line through $P$ parallel to $m$, forcing $l = n$ and generating a contradiction.
